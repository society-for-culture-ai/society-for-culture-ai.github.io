The Society for
Culture and AI

We are a group of researchers who have long worked in the broad space of culture and AI. We are announcing today a new society of scholars, with a first annual conference in late 2027.

Motivation

As AI-based chatbots and tools become more popular, their effects on human culture have grown, leading to both urgent societal challenges and fresh research interest in topics with roots in computational social science, cultural analytics, the digital humanities, and human-computer interaction. These disciplines have long histories that need to be in conversation with new subfields, theories, and communities emerging in response to AI.

We can observe this “cultural turn” through the following frames.

  1. Cultural Alignment and Adaptability in AI

    AI models need to be knowledgeable and adaptable to diverse cultural contexts (countries, social groups, online communities), while understanding the complex interplay between culture and language, and adapting responsibly to avoid profiling or erasure.

  2. AI as Cultural Technology

    Like libraries or the printing press, language models are cultural technologies that re-organize human knowledge and can be used as a lens into human culture.

  3. AI’s Effects on Culture

    From the publishing industry to social media to cultural heritage institutions, the widespread use of AI is causing cultural disruption, which needs to be documented and studied.

  4. AI as Cultural Analytics Tool

    AI’s new capabilities provide stronger tools than ever before to study human cultural data at scale, including large datasets of films, books, music, news articles, social media, and games.

Because this work doesn’t have a natural cross-disciplinary home, the community is split across many disciplines, conferences, and publication venues. Conversations that should intersect are instead siloed along traditional disciplinary borders, newcomers can’t easily benefit from prior work, and we face difficulties (such as overlapping work) in constructing shared methods and theories.

Plan

Researchers of culture and AI urgently need an organizing structure and a central conference venue to converse and coordinate. This structure should be welcoming to scholars from computer science, the humanities, the social sciences, and more — to anyone using AI to study culture, anyone studying the cultural effects of AI, and anyone designing AI models for cultural competency.

We believe this new in-person, annual convening should have the following qualities:

  1. A “big tent” philosophy that brings different disciplines into conversation, while providing space for micro-conferences and collaborations
  2. An openness to new methods while grounded in foundational prior theory and results
  3. Focus on open and transparent research
  4. Creative reviewing structures that accommodate increasing submission loads
  5. Open-access publications associated with presentation
  6. Flexibility to the changing environment

With these goals in mind, we are founding the Society for Culture and AI, with a first annual Conference on Culture and AI (CAI), to be held in late 2027 at Princeton University.

People

We represent research groups from academic institutions around the world, each of which has long been working at the intersection of culture and computation. Our training and disciplines vary widely; traditionally, we have sought appointments in different departments, attended separate conferences, and published in separate venues. We are united in our common concerns about AI and culture and our common commitment to building collaborative futures for AI through deep inter-disciplinary understanding.
